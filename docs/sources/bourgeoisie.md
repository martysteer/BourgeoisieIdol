# Bourgeoisie

**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie

**Theme:** Class systems, social dynamics

## Definition

In capitalist society, Marx identified the bourgeoisie as the capitalist class, which owns the means of production. More specifically, in the strictly Marxist sense, the bourgeoisie represents those who own the means of production, and they earn profits by deriving work from workers, while the workers sell their ability to work and their labor for a wage.

## Key Characteristics in Marxist Theory

The bourgeoisie are traditionally contrasted with the proletariat by their wealth, political power, and education, as well as their access to and control of cultural, social, and financial capital.

The relationship between capitalists and workers is inherently exploitative, as the capitalists extract surplus value from the workers' labor.

## Class Struggle

Marx posited that history is fundamentally a story of class conflict, in which the ruling class, which owns and controls the means of production, exploits the subordinate classes who do not.

## Historical Role

According to the Marxist view of history, during the 17th and 18th centuries, the bourgeoisie were the politically progressive social class who supported the principles of constitutional government and of natural right, though Marx later saw them as becoming increasingly reactionary under capitalism.
