# Making of A Brief Visual Exploration of A Dictionary of Typography

**Source:** https://www.c82.net/blog/?id=86

**Project:** https://www.c82.net/typography/

**Theme:** Historical semantics, visual timelines, evolution of technical language

## About the Source Material

John Southward was a printer in Liverpool who wrote **A Dictionary of Typography and its Accessory Arts** as parts that appeared in the Printers' Register from 1870 to 1871. In 1875, a second revised edition was published as a stand-alone publication.

## The Project

Created by Nicholas Rougeux, this is a **short visual exploration of the differences between the two editions** of Southward's Dictionary viewed as a single alphabetical list of terms. It's a visual exploration of the structure throughout both editions as well as a complete reproduction of both dictionaries, including:

- Visualizations of references mentioned
- How the first edition evolved into the second with commentary
- A full list of all the terms
- Posters featuring visual representations of every term defined in both editions

## Key Differences Between Editions

The first edition included beautiful intricate illustrations for:
- Composing machines
- Degener's press
- Duncan's press
- And more...

**These were left out and described in less detail in the second edition.**

Other differences include:
- Minor grammatical changes
- Addition or removal of short histories
- Moving parts of definitions from one entry to another
  - Example: Annals of printing had a timeline of the history of printing that was moved to "Printing" in the second edition

## Methodology

The project tracks how typographic terminology evolved between 1871 and 1875, showing additions, deletions, and transformations in both textual definitions and visual illustrations. This creates a **visual semantic timeline** demonstrating how technical language develops through practice.
